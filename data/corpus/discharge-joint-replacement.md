# Going home after a joint replacement

Keep the wound dressing dry and leave it in place until the practice nurse changes it.
Showering is usually allowed after five days with a waterproof cover. Do not soak the
wound in a bath until the wound has fully closed.

Take the pain relief you were given on a regular schedule for the first two weeks rather
than waiting until the pain is bad. Pain relief taken regularly lets you do the
physiotherapy, and the physiotherapy is what restores the joint.

Do your prescribed exercises three times a day. Some ache and some swelling during and
after the exercises is expected for the first six weeks. Ice wrapped in a towel for
twenty minutes helps the swelling settle.

Walk a little and often. Use the frame or the crutches you were given until the
physiotherapist says otherwise. Avoid crossing your legs and avoid twisting on the
operated leg.

Contact the service before your follow-up if the wound leaks fluid, if the skin around it
changes, or if the calf becomes painful or swollen. Those are the changes the surgical
team wants to review promptly.

Your wound check is at ten days and your first physiotherapy review is at two weeks. Most
people stop using crutches somewhere between four and six weeks, but this varies and is
not a race.
