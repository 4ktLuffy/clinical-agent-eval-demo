# Going home after pneumonia

Finish the whole course of antibiotics even if you feel better after a few days. Stopping
early is the most common reason a chest infection comes back. Take each dose with a glass
of water and at roughly the same times each day.

Tiredness after pneumonia is normal and lasts longer than most people expect. Six weeks of
feeling below par is usual. Build activity back gradually rather than all at once. A short
walk each day is better than one long walk a week.

Drink more fluid than usual while the cough is settling. A cough can persist for up to six
weeks after the infection has cleared, and a dry cough at that stage is not a sign of
failure. Keep the room ventilated and avoid smoke.

If you were given an inhaler, use it with the spacer you were shown on the ward. Rinse
your mouth after a steroid inhaler. Bring the inhaler and the spacer to your follow-up so
the nurse can watch your technique.

Contact the service before your follow-up appointment if the fever returns, if the cough
brings up blood, or if you become breathless doing something you managed comfortably the
day before. Those three changes are the ones the ward team wants to hear about early.

Your follow-up chest review is usually six weeks after discharge. Bring your medicines
list with you. If you smoke, the stop smoking service can be added to that appointment.
